\begin{enumerate}
	\item Channel efficiency = Hydraulic radius -> The higher the radius, the greater the efficiency.
	\item $\text{Cross-Sectional Area}\div\text{Wetted Perimeter} = \text{Hydraulic Radius}$
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Channel Width}

\begin{figure}[H]
	\centering
	\includegraphics[width=0.75\textwidth, angle=90]{Images/width_1.jpg}
\end{figure}

\begin{enumerate}
	\item To measure the width use a tape measure.
	\item It should be perpendicular to river flow and the points should be at the riverbanks where the water meets the bank (should be across surface).
	\item Pull the ends taut before taking measurements.
	\item Measure five times for accuracy.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{enumerate}
	\item To measure depth use either a meter rule or ranging pole. Meter rule is better for accuracy. 
	\item The narrower side should be exposed to river flow. Measure distance between water surface and riverbed along a tape measure.
	\item  More measurements = higher accuracy. Use average of 5 measurements.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{River Discharge}

%\includegraphics[width=0.7\textwidth]{Images/xx.jpg}\par\vspace{1.5cm}

\begin{enumerate}
	\item $\text{Discharge (cumecs)}= \text{Cross-Sectional Area}\ (\si{\metre\squared}) \times \text{Velocity}\ (\si{\metre\per\second})$
	\item Use mean depth and mean width to measure cross-sectional area (\si{\metre\squared}).
	\item Measure velocity either through digital flow-meter or with a stopwatch, a cork,measuring tape and ranging poles ($\times 4$)
	\item Then use the average velocity and multiply it by the area to get the discharge
\end{enumerate}

\begin{enumerate}
	\item Use systematic sampling to choose the rocks
	\item Use a calliper to measure the size of the rocks by focusing on length, width and breadth
	\item Repeat multiple times and record results in a table
	\item Calculate average 
\end{enumerate}
