\section{Evaluation}

The strengths of this investigation was that measurements were taken by a specific group of people which allowed for uniformity in the results as the same people measured a specific data set. Pragmatic and Systematic sampling techniques were used, which allowed for more accurate results. Sites that were easily accessible were also chosen, to allow for a more accurate use of fieldwork techniques. 

On the the other hand there were also many weaknesses, as some of the equipment used were not ideal for certain measurements, such as the tape measure which was used to measure the wetted perimeter. Furthermore, purposive sampling was also utilised and areas covered with shrubs, algae or large rocks were avoided, forming a biased data set. Furthermore, the time of the year and the time span within which the data was gathered was also highly influential as the water table during summer is lower compared to winter times, and gathering this data on a sunny day yields results different to rainy days. All of these combined form moderate to high biased data sets with room for error. 

The Bradshaw model is not ideal for European rivers because it is too simple and assumes everything changes smoothly from the source to the mouth. In reality, European rivers are heavily changed by humans for example, they have dams, weirs, and controlled channels. They are also affected by things like snowmelt, changing seasons, and different land types across the basin. These real-world factors mean European rivers do not follow the neat patterns shown in the Bradshaw model, which makes the model unrealistic for this region \parencite{seibert2022hbv}.

A better model for European rivers is the HBV model. This model works well because it includes important processes like soil moisture, snow accumulation and melt, groundwater flow, and rainfall–runoff responses. It can also handle differences across the river basin. Studies show that HBV performs very well in large European rivers such as the Rhine. In fact, research found HBV to be more accurate than another advanced model when tested on the Rhine basin \parencite{telinde2008rhine}. This makes HBV a much more suitable choice than the Bradshaw model.
