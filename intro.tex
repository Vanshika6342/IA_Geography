\subsection{Bradshaw Model}

\begin{figure}[H]
	\centering
	\includegraphics[width=0.65\textwidth]{Images/Bradshaw_Model.png}
	\caption{Bradshaw Model \parencite{internetgeography_riverfieldwork}}
\end{figure}

The Bradshaw Model is a geographical model which theorizes the characteristics of a river, stream, or tributary as one moves downstream from the source to the mouth \parencite{enacademic_weir}. However, the model can be read from both ways, upstream and downstream. Here it portrays how river discharge, channel width, depth, average velocity, and load quantity increase as one proceeds downstream. On the other hand, moving down stream, the load particle size, channel bed roughness, and gradient decrease \parencite{coolgeography_channelcharacteristics}. Despite showing the predicted river characteristics moving downstream, the Bradshaw Model does not encompass all the features of the river. Properties such as channel efficiency, river valley shapes and river landforms are exempt from this paradigm.

\subsection{Aim}

The aim of this following research is to determine the accuracy of the Bradshaw Model in predicting the characteristics of a river as it flows from its source down to its mouth. In modern times, many anthropogenic changes to natural landforms have been made, either to accommodate growing settlements or to make way for various projects at an ideal, specific location. To evaluate the impact of these modifications on, and whether all rivers do act in accordance to, the Bradshaw model, the following question, \textbf{\textit{``To what extent does the River Modau conform to the Bradshaw Model?''}}, was formulated. 

The survey for this study was conducted on the 26th of June, 2025 between the hours 8 a.m. to 5 p.m.. This investigation adheres to Option A: Freshwater, specifically\textbf{ Drainage Basin Hydrology} and \textbf{Geomorphology} \parencite{clastify2023}.


\subsection{Modau River}

The Modau River is a small river located in the southern part of the state of Hesse, Germany. Geographically, it originates in the Odenwald hills near Neunkirchen and flows northwest through a series of valleys and lowlands. 

The river stretches approximately 44 kilometres before it empties into the Rhine River near Stockstadt am Rhine. As it flows through town like Ober-Ramstadt, Pfungstadt, and parts of Darmstadt, the Modau shapes the surrounding landscape by contributing to the local drainage system and influencing soil fertility.

Its drainage basin lies within a temperate climate zone, and the river plays a role in regional hydrology, especially in terms of groundwater surface-water runoff.

\section{Hypotheses}
\label{sec:hypotheses}

\textbf{Hypothesis 1:} If one moves downstream, then the \textbf{velocity}\footnote{The speed at which the river flows measured in either \SI{}{\meter\per\second} or \SI{}{\kilo\meter\per\second}.} increases as \textbf{channel efficiency} increases. 
	
This is as channel efficiency is entirely dependent on the \textbf{hydraulic radius.}\footnote{The cross-sectional area divided by the wetted perimeter provides the hydraulic radius.} An increase in hydraulic radius leads to an increase in channel efficiency, which means the proportion of water in contact with the river banks and bed is less, meaning there is less drag, allowing the velocity to increase. 
	
\begin{figure}[H]
	\centering
	\includegraphics[width=0.6\textwidth]{Images/ChannelEfficiency.png}
	\caption{Channel Efficiency example \parencite{slideshare2023}.}
\end{figure}
	
As portrayed above, a semi-circular channel shape provides less contact with the river banks and bed compared to a flat floored channel, meaning the semi-circular channel has a higher channel efficiency. Furthermore, there is more vertical and lateral erosion as one moves downstream, increasing channel efficiency as the channel walls get smoother, causing less friction.
	

	
\textbf{Hypothesis 2:} If one moves downstream, then there is an increase in \textbf{river discharge}\footnote{The volume of water flowing through a channel at a certain point of time, measured in cumecs (\SI{}{\cubic\meter\per\second}).}, because \textbf{channel width}\footnote{How wide the river is, measured laterally, in meters, at the surface from river bank to river bank where the water touches the land.} and \textbf{channel depth}\footnote{How deep the river bed is from the surface of the water, measured at set intervals perpendicular to river flow in order to account for the irregular shape of the river bed, measured in meters.} increase.

This occurs as the more downstream one goes, the more the water erodes the banks and the river bed through abrasion due to its sediments, increasing channel depth and width. This increases the carrying capacity of the river, allowing for a greater river discharge. 



\textbf{Hypothesis 3:} \textbf{Bedload}\footnote{The sediment transported along the bottom of the river due to the river flow \parencite{fiveable2023}.} \textbf{size} will decrease as we go downstream, as there are more erosional processes taking place due to the increased amount of sediment being carried in the river.

This is reasonable as due to an increased amount of load carried by the river as it moves downstream, there are more erosional processes taking place and to a greater extent. These include processes like traction, corrosion, abrasion, and more where sediment drags along the river bed eroding itself and the bed of the river, acidic conditions in the water can cause sediments like limestone to erode, small rocks hitting each other due to the river current causing them to wear each other down, and more.
